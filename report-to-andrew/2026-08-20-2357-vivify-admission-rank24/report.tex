\section*{Objective and outcome}
The objective was to start the next immutable Tier-D closure at rank 24,
compare Tactix and Kissat from the latest common semantic state, and repair
only the earliest portable scheduling, representation, or semantic
divergence.  The investigation specifically audited Vivify admission,
cadence, candidate ordering, tier shares, and its placement among adjacent
proof rules.

The accepted repair removes every formula-selected Vivify disable.  It is a
general source-parity correction: formula classification may dose BVE,
Factor, forward work, and Vivify effort, but cannot remove the Vivify
opportunity that Kissat offers in every search-time Probe phase.  The repair
closes rank 24 UNSAT in 356.205 seconds with an independently verified proof.
The cumulative ranks-21--40 gate preserves all six inherited closures and
also closes rank 34 SAT and rank 39 UNSAT.  The accepted set is now ranks 21,
23, 24, 25, 28, 29, 34, 38, and 39; eleven ranks remain open.

\section*{Root cause and design decision}
The immutable rank-24 formula contains 167,755 variables and 473,169 clauses.
The old formula classifier placed every formula in the 120,000--199,999
variable range into a \code{NoVivify} profile.  During scheduler construction,
that profile omitted the Vivify entry from all search-time Probe phases.

Kissat has no equivalent variable-range or density-range admission policy.
Its \code{probe.c} calls \code{kissat\_vivify} unconditionally between the
early Backbone and Sweep calls in every search-time Probe.  The
\code{vivify} option, 100-per-mille effort, 3/3/1 redundant-tier shares,
irredundant share, and irredundant productivity delay bound the work inside
that opportunity.  Kissat's reduced startup Probe intentionally omits
Vivify.  Thus the earliest portable divergence was policy admission at the
first due search-time Probe, not a Vivify rule-side guard.

The classifier's former \code{*NoVivify} variants now collapse onto the same
profiles with only their independently supported BVE, Factor, forward, and
dose dimensions retained.  No benchmark name, hash, rank, deadline, literal,
conflict, per-instance arm, or new tuned constant appears in the solver.
Explicit typed configuration and \code{TACTIX\_SLOT\_DISABLE=Vivify} remain
available as diagnostic and N1 selection surfaces.

\section*{Vivify parity audit}
The repair is admission-only because the surrounding machinery is already
source-aligned and regression-pinned.  The exact default orders are:

\begin{description}
\item[Startup Probe.] Congruence, Substitute, Backbone, Sweep, Substitute,
Factor.
\item[Search-time Probe.] Congruence, Substitute, Backbone, Vivify, Sweep,
Substitute, TransitiveReduce, Backbone, Factor.
\end{description}

Within Vivify, Tactix uses the focused dynamic tier limits; 3/3/1 redundant
shares followed by the irredundant share; Kissat's leftover priority and
whole-scope re-arm; occurrence-ranked candidate and literal order with the
descending raw-literal tie; committed clause pose and rebuilt large watches;
cross-candidate trail-prefix reuse; earliest implied-literal selection;
deduction precedence; and the one-percent irredundant productivity delay.
The stale source comment suggesting that \code{better\_implied} was absent
was corrected: the later trail-order scan already implements it.

\code{test\_scheduling} now pins both complete default sequences.
\code{test\_strategy\_config} sweeps every formula-profile boundary and
requires default Vivify admission, while separately proving that a caller's
explicit disable remains intact.  Existing focused Vivify order, deduction,
tier, N1, and proof tests remain unchanged.

Diagnostic Slurm job \code{1636836} ran the frozen rank-24 input through
instrumented Kissat and the preceding Tactix source with symmetric trace
controls.  Both diagnostic tasks timed out at 1,200 seconds and left no
certificate residue.  The trace identifies Kissat's search-time Vivify
boundary; the source classifier identifies why Tactix could not take it.  No
frozen reference performance result was rerun.

\section*{Limitations, risks, and next actions}
This repair does not claim exact runtime parity with Kissat, and it does not
erase formula-profile dosing for independently measured mechanisms.  One
Tier-B instance slows substantially, although aggregate B and C time improves
and coverage is unchanged.  Candidate-order and deduction parity remain
subject to their existing ledger reopening conditions.

Rank 22 remains parked at its previously localized common-input Vivify
boundary; this admission repair does not by itself close that much larger
instance.  Following the requested UNSAT-first order, the next open targets
are ranks 27, 31, 32, 33, 35, 37, and 40.  Each future change still owes the
full Tier-B plus predeclared mechanism-relevant Tier-C guard before its Tier-D
witness.  Reopen this repair if any formula classifier again clears
\code{vivify\_enabled}, if either default Probe sequence changes, if a checked
predecessor is lost, or if proof/model validation or cleanup fails.
