\section*{Executive outcome}
The suspicious Vivify variable-range policy was not present in Kissat and has
been removed.  The follow-on comparison now also closes the residual Sweep
scheduling mismatch that remained after the semantic-dose and arena-lifecycle
repair.  Tactix used a strict-overrun predicate inside its Kitten-mode
sub-solver where Kissat stops at equality.  That one tick moved seed 9080 from
the opening Sweep session into the resumed session, reset the deterministic
Kitten phase stream at a different point, and made later equivalence and
representative orders look independently wrong.

The production repair is general: Kitten-mode decision and Sweep outer
refinement polls are inclusive, exactly where Kissat's source uses
\code{>=}.  The enclosing Sweep seed loop remains strict \code{>}, also as in
Kissat, and BVE's older entry-counter budget keeps its existing strict contract.
No formula-size range, benchmark identity, literal, rank, deadline, or tuned
constant enters solver behavior.

The repaired candidate matches the retained Kissat trace for all 595 opening
seeds, all 2,303 resumed seeds, and all 343 equivalence identities in discovery
order.  Release passes 72/72.  The required matched 28B+5C guard passes 66/66,
all models and proofs validate, and residue is zero.  Immutable rank 27 still
returns UNKNOWN at 1,200.004 seconds, so rank 27 remains open.

\section*{Why one comparator changed the whole schedule}
The first candidate-only retained-tail trace, job \code{1637760}, showed that
the opening Sweep sessions were identical for 594 seeds.  Retained Kissat then
visited external seed 9080; Tactix stopped and retained it.  At conflict 3069,
Tactix began with 9080 and visited 2,306 seeds, versus Kissat's 2,303 beginning
at 9132.

Tactix ended the opening call at 1,000,001 embedded ticks.  The relevant
reference source has two different boundary rules:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Kitten decision / Sweep refinement:} & \mathrm{ticks}\geq\mathrm{limit}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{stop},\\
\text{enclosing Sweep seed loop:} & \mathrm{ticks}>\mathrm{limit}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{stop}.
\end{array}
\]
Tactix had used the second predicate inside Kitten as well.  At equality it
made one additional decision, propagated to 1,000,001, and therefore correctly
failed the enclosing test before seed 9080.  Kissat stops embedded search at
exactly 1,000,000, sees that the enclosing strict-overrun test is still false,
and visits 9080 in the same session.

Session placement is semantic, not accounting trivia.  One Sweep invocation
owns one embedded Kitten generator; clearing an environment advances it, while
starting a new invocation resets it.  Moving 9080 across that boundary shifted
the phase/model stream for every later environment.  The previously observed
four-way equivalence order and representative rescheduling differences were
therefore downstream consequences, not separate stack-order defects.

\section*{Implementation and focused evidence}
The source change is deliberately small.

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{SubSolver::solve} uses \code{spent >= budget} only for
  \code{SubTickMode::Kitten}.  The entry meter retains \code{>}.
\item Sweep polls \code{>=} before and after whole backbone/partition flip
  passes, matching \code{kitten\_ticks\_limit\_hit}.
\item Inner flip loops and the enclosing seed loop retain their source-backed
  strict-overrun checks.
\end{itemize}

A focused unit test imports three variables with one ternary clause, reaches
the Kitten budget exactly after the first decision propagation, and requires
Budget before a second decision.  The pre-repair comparator returned SAT after
doing the extra work.  No soundness failure or crash occurred, so the plan's
Claude repair-delegation trigger did not fire.
