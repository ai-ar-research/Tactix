\section{Outcome}

The Rank-4 gap is reduced from 1.9850x to 1.3888x relative to current
instrumented Kissat by repairing one portable Factor lifecycle mismatch.  The
accepted predecessor took a stable median 15.340 seconds, the candidate takes
10.733 seconds, and current Kissat takes 7.728 seconds.  All three arms have
under-0.66\% spread over three paired node018 waves.  The candidate is about
30.0\% faster than its predecessor.

This is a search-quality recovery rather than a micro-optimization.  The
Factor rule still takes about 30 ms, but restoring one missing accepted grid
reduces conflicts from 983,053 to 647,074, close to Kissat's 651,764.  Exact
tracing establishes identical heap order, candidate order, accepted grids,
ticks, and early learned state after the repair.

The complete B28+C5 correctness gate and the one Claude-owned fuzz campaign
both pass.  No correctness repair was needed.  The increment is therefore
ready for promotion, after which Rank 32 is the next declared witness.

The two Factor implementations accept exactly the same first 284 grids.  At
the next heap frontier, both pop external literal 521 at the same position,
with score 5 and the same surrounding heap state.  Kissat admits it and applies
grid \code{[521,-31,-32]}; Tactix discards it because its local per-pass

heap order are otherwise equal.

Source tracing identifies the missing lifecycle transition.  Adding the
divider and quotient clauses marks both polarities of Kissat's new fresh
variable.  Tactix re-armed the pre-existing factors and remainders, but did not
copy those two fresh clause-add marks into the local pass image.  This is the
first demonstrated worse-side owner; it is not a tick, heap, or candidate-order
difference.

\section{Portable repair and semantic parity}

After an accepted composite grid adds its one fresh variable, Tactix now
mirrors the already-authoritative marks for its positive and negative literals
into the local \code{armed} image.  It does not push either literal merely
because it was created.  A later touched-candidate update controls scheduling,
exactly as in Kissat.  No benchmark identity, rank, deadline, or tuned constant
is present, and the proof-rule/strategy boundary and N1--N4 remain unchanged.

The frozen candidate and exact-tick campaigns establish parity for:

\begin{itemize}
  \item 1,251 Factor heap pops and 1,309 viable seeds;
  \item 381 accepted grids and all 1,971 ordered grid members;
  \item 1,562 heap pop/update observations; and
  \item exactly 340,150 Factor ticks in each solver.
\end{itemize}

After normalizing internal clause IDs and the implementations' distinct
inprocessing-propagation accounting, the first nine decisions, first conflict
order/set/levels, raw through final learned clause, retained counts, search
choice, and top-32 order are also exact.  All 76 Release tests pass.  The
tracked Kissat trace overlay cleanly rematerializes, configures, and compiles
from the pinned source.
