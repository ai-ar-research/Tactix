\section*{Executive outcome}
The suspicious variable-range Vivify policy was real and has been removed.
Vivify now follows the retained Kissat evidence for candidate scope, cumulative
tier allowance, jumped-reason deduction, terminal effects, productivity, and
its interleaving with the surrounding Probe pipeline.  The first later portable
divergences were then repaired in sequence: TransitiveReduce source order and
Sweep's semantic-dose/representation lifecycle.

The Sweep defect was a category error rather than another benchmark gate.
Tactix charged a whole-arena representation rebuild to the current live Sweep
allowance, while Kissat's source limits that call only by embedded
\code{kitten\_ticks}.  The rebuild ended a productive retained tail after the
first equivalence batch.  Tactix now uses Kitten ticks for the live semantic
ceiling, continues to price representation work in later scheduler accounting,
and carries one dense substitution ledger across productive environments before
collecting the arena once at call exit.

Release passes 72/72.  The required matched 28B+5C guard passes 66/66 with all
models and proofs checked and zero certificate residue.  The first meter-only
candidate had timed out on the fixed Tier-C \code{T105.2.0} guard; the complete
lifecycle repair proves it UNSAT in 1,043.578 seconds.  Immutable rank 27 still
returns UNKNOWN at 1,200.004 seconds, so rank 27 remains open.

\section*{What was wrong}
At the common conflict-3069 state, retained Kissat evidence continues the
second Sweep call through 2,303 resumed seeds and derives 343 equivalences.
Tactix's original live meter was
\[
  \texttt{sweep\_ticks\_sub}+\texttt{sweep\_ticks\_repr}.
\]
After the first productive environment, Tactix crossed its variable-width
arena bridge.  That one collection charged roughly 18.8 million representation
words against a 19.19-million semantic dose and stopped the call.  Kissat has
no corresponding charge: \code{sweep.c} sets and tests the outer allowance in
cumulative \code{kitten\_ticks} alone.

Separating the live meter restored the semantic tail exactly, but initially
exposed a second host-representation cost: Tactix collected the entire arena
after every productive environment.  The resulting call charged
1,100,956,356 representation words, took 53.072 seconds, and failed the full
guard.  This failed guard correctly blocked a Tier-D witness.

The existing \code{SweepSubstitutionSession} supplied the general repair.  Its
ordered dense occurrence ledger is updated by every substitution transaction,
so the next environment already sees the previous environment's consequences.
The dense call therefore needs one collection at exit, not one collection per
productive environment.  Sessionless/public calls retain immediate collection
because they own raw arena references and must refresh them after relocation.

\section*{Implementation and focused evidence}
The source change has three parts.

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{sweep\_semantic\_effort} is the cumulative Kitten counter used by
  the current call's ceiling and remaining per-environment allowance.
\item \code{sweep\_effort} remains Kitten ticks plus representation words for
  policy scheduling, so real host work is not hidden.
\item A connected dense call collects once after its ordered environment
  sequence; a sessionless call keeps its immediate collection and generation
  refresh.
\end{itemize}

No benchmark name, hash, rank, literal, deadline, variable range, or tuned
constant enters solver behavior.  A focused two-environment test requires at
least two equivalences and substitutions but exactly one arena generation
increment under a connected dense index.  The existing sessionless test pins
the opposite correctness-required lifecycle.
