\section{Objective and outcome}

The objective is unchanged: bring Tactix within 10\,\% of Kissat's wall time on
Tier~D Ranks~1--40 while preserving N1--N4, with B28+C5 as the regression gate.
Kissat's reference (\code{1930}) is 7{,}675.1~s, so the target is 8{,}442.7~s.

\textbf{Outcome.} Four commits were gated and pushed; \code{origin/main} is
\code{2c30fd3}. Ranks~1--40 stand at \textbf{8{,}738.8~s}, which is
\textbf{1.138$\times$} Kissat, and B28+C5 is \textbf{0.491}. The headline moved
little this session, and the reason matters more than the number: every one of
the forty conflict counts is identical to the previous gate, so the change is
behaviour-preserving and the $\pm$14~s between the two totals is
node-assignment noise rather than a measurement of the work landed.

The substantive result is diagnostic. Sweep is the largest single per-rule gap
left on Tier~D, and this session established exactly what that gap is made of.
It is not search quality, not the embedded solver, and not any one hot line.
It is that Tactix applies a discovered equivalence through a general
substitution path that searches a list per clause, where Kissat walks one list
per literal.

\section{What the measurement says}

Rank~22 spends 51.7~s in Sweep against Kissat's 12.9~s. The two do almost the
same work.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & Tactix & Kissat \\
\midrule
variables swept        & 221{,}816 & 196{,}771 \\
encoded clauses        & 47.6\,M   & 43.2\,M \\
equivalences found     & 119{,}339 & 119{,}987 \\
units found            & 1{,}072   & 1{,}118 \\
sub-solver ticks       & 678\,M    & 654\,M \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every counter agrees within 10\,\%, and the ticks agree within 4\,\%. So this
is a unit-cost gap at matched work, not a scheduling or dosing difference.

The embedded solver is not the cause either. In a paired source-line profile
against a Kissat built with symbols, Tactix's sub-solver carries 1.30\,\% of
samples where Kissat's \code{kitten} carries 2.40\,\%. Tactix's sub-solver is
the faster of the two.

A call-graph profile then splits Sweep's 17.36\,\% inclusive share cleanly:
finding the equivalences costs 2.0\,\%, and applying them costs 11.74\,\%.
Roughly seven-eighths of Sweep is the write-back.

\section{Why the write-back costs what it does}

Kissat applies one equivalence with \code{substitute\_connected\_clauses}. That
is a single compacting walk of the retired literal's dense list: each clause is
rewritten in the arena in place, keeps its reference, and the moved watches are
appended to the representative in one further pass. Nothing is searched.

Tactix builds a list of binary images, compacts the affected owners, pushes the
images, replaces each large clause in place, and reconciles a parallel ledger
that reproduces Kissat's single mixed watch stream on Tactix's separate
indexes. Three steps of that path search a list once per clause: replacing a
large clause searches the retired literal's occurrence vector, searches its
ordered stream, and compacts both watch lists. Each search is linear in a
degree that is itself the number of clauses already rewritten onto that
representative, because a substituted class sends every retired literal's
clauses to the same place. The cost is therefore quadratic in the class size.

\section{What was repaired}

Two instances of that pattern were removed, both verified by identical solver
counters on interleaved same-node repeats.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & Sweep & Rank 22 total \\
\midrule
baseline                          & 52.2~s & 292.7~s \\
incremental ledger republish      & 51.7~s & 291.5~s \\
duplicate test grouped by owner   & 48.8~s & 287.1~s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The ledger previously reconciled and republished every literal the whole Sweep
call had ever touched, once per emission batch, which makes one environment pay
for every earlier environment's footprint. It now carries the set of streams a
batch actually mutated. A debug oracle re-derives the full publication on every
batch and asserts the incremental one already matched it; it holds across the
whole test suite.

The duplicate test was the clearer instance. Every rewritten binary asked
whether its target pair was already live by scanning the target owner's binary
prefix, from both endpoints. Grouping the images by owner and scanning each
distinct owner once answers every image identically. A debug assertion
re-derives the original per-image answer, and it caught a real error in the
first attempt: the preceding compaction leaves a pair present in one endpoint's
list and absent from the other, so both directions genuinely have to be asked.

\section{Limitations and what was refuted}

The tier headline did not move outside noise, and it would be wrong to present
these repairs as having moved it. Rank~22 improved by 5.6~s in paired repeats;
the tier total rose by 14.3~s with every conflict count unchanged. Both
statements are true and the second is noise.

One further attempt measured neutral and was reverted rather than committed.
Rank~21's elimination profile carries 2.9\,\% in \code{push\_back} and 2.2\,\%
in \code{free} where Kissat's carries neither, and BVE's occurrence audit
constructed a literal vector for every clause it visits across 3.59\,M
candidate eliminations. Hoisting that vector and two others out of their loops
kept every counter identical and changed the run by nothing measurable, so the
allocator is not where that rank's elimination time goes.

Two documentation budgets are over: \code{PROGRESS.md} is 3{,}773 lines against
350, and \code{docs/implementation-notes.md} is 591 against 560. Compaction was
not requested, so the debt is reported rather than paid.

\section{Next concrete action}

Give Sweep Kissat's connected walk in place of the general substitution path.
The measurement above says what that is worth: about 36~s on Rank~22 alone,
which is the largest single per-rule gap remaining on Tier~D. The work is to
replace the image-list-plus-ledger write-back with one compacting pass over the
retired literal's stream, rewriting each clause in the arena and appending
moved watches to the representative, so that no step is linear in the
representative's growing degree.

Evidence for every number here is in
\code{benchmarks/reports/2026-09-02-unit-cost-and-fidelity-stack.md},
sections~3.12 and~3.13, with campaigns \code{5010} through \code{5016}.
