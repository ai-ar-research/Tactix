\section{Objective and outcome}

The objective is to close every identified implementation gap against Kissat:
every case where Tactix performs the same work and takes longer. Being within
10\,\% on Tier~D Ranks~1--40 is the floor, not the finish. B28+C5 is the
regression gate and N1--N4 are preserved.

\textbf{Outcome.} Ranks~1--40 moved from 8{,}781.1~s to \textbf{8{,}535~s},
which is \textbf{1.112$\times$} Kissat's 7{,}675.1~s, and B28+C5 from 0.495 to
\textbf{0.463}. Nine repairs landed, each gated 40/40 valid with 10{,}000 fuzz
cases and zero failures on both legs. Every one is behaviour-preserving: solver
counters are identical between arms on every instance measured, and on all
forty gate ranks. None of them changes the search.

The net per-rule gap against Kissat fell from roughly 500~s to \textbf{428~s}.

\section{What was repaired}

\textbf{A diagnostic that shipped.} BVE ran an occurrence audit on every
candidate elimination: both polarities' entire occurrence streams walked, every
large clause dereferenced, each scanned twice, a literal vector built per
clause. Its only consumers are two trace calls that are empty inline functions
in a production build. Compiling it out is worth \textbf{170.6~s} across the
tier, the largest single repair of the project.

\textbf{Factor, at provably matched work.} Kissat charges 733\,M factor ticks on
Rank~22 and Tactix 764\,M in the same currency, for 8.9~s against 25.2~s. Per
scanned entry Tactix took two random cache misses where Kissat takes one,
because Kissat's skip mark is a clause header bit read from the line it already
loads. Moving both marks into the header, which then made the occurrence
identifier dead and let the watch record fall from 16 bytes to 8, plus replacing
the remainder pool's map of bucket vectors with an open-addressed table, took
Factor from 281.8~s to 238~s tier-wide.

\textbf{Congruence's doubled ITE gates.} Kissat canonicalises an ITE triple
before storing a gate, so one entry covers a relation and its output-negated
twin. Tactix keyed on the sequence as found and kept both. Rank~22's gate count
falls from 10.48\,M to 7.10\,M against Kissat's 6.91\,M, the merge count is
unchanged at 1{,}543{,}122, and every counter is identical.

\textbf{Four quadratic removals.} Substitution retires a literal from every
clause holding it, so its occurrence list is consumed whole; removing entries
one clause at a time scanned 40\,G elements on Rank~22. The root-satisfied
binary scans recounted each pair through the watch list. The binary rewrite's
duplicate test rescanned the representative's prefix per image. And removing a
literal searched the large list and the ordered stream separately for the same
reference. Each is now one pass.

\section{What was refuted}

Four attempts measured neutral or unsound and were dropped rather than
committed: accumulating Factor's scan charge in a local, guarding fourteen
trace-only computations that the compiler already eliminates, hoisting BVE's
per-clause vectors, and bulk-clearing an eliminated variable's binaries, whose
precondition fails in the rule's own tests.

Two negative results matter as much as the repairs. Propagation is 42\,\% of
runtime and looked 1.5$\times$ slower on Rank~31, but that instance runs
2.7$\times$ Kissat's conflicts and so carries longer watch lists; at equal
propagation counts on Rank~22 the rates are within 9\,\%. And Sweep, which an
earlier report named the largest remaining gap on the strength of one rank, is
120~s \emph{faster} than Kissat across the tier.

\section{A correction to how we measure}

Two measurement errors were found and fixed, and both changed conclusions.

The hand-written A/B campaigns ran at four CPUs and 16~GiB, which the benchmark
runner documents as the \emph{debug} contract; the gates use twelve and 48~GiB.
A bandwidth-sensitive result read at the wrong allocation is not evidence. The
two-word Factor watch record was refuted under those conditions and, retried
correctly after the cache-miss confound was removed, proved worth 2.8~s on
Rank~22.

More consequentially, the tier total's noise band is far wider than the 50~s
this project had assumed. The final repair measured a consistent $-1.4$~s on
Rank~22 across interleaved repeats and then returned a gate \textbf{72.8~s
slower} with all forty conflict counts identical to the previous gate. The
change cannot add work, so that is machine variation. The tier total is a
validity check and a coarse trend; it cannot arbitrate a behaviour-identical
change. Paired same-node repeats must.

Read Strengthen's row with care: Kissat runs forward subsumption inside
\code{eliminate}, not in its \code{subsume} phase, so the row overstates the
gap. The fair pairing is BVE plus Strengthen against eliminate plus subsume,
372~s against 230~s. Tactix does 12\,\% \emph{fewer} forward steps and only
8\,\% more eliminations, so that 142~s is unit cost and it is the largest
single gap pairing left.

Searching for more instances of the ITE pattern, a Tactix work counter
exceeding Kissat's for the same outcome, found none in BVE or Strengthen. The
remaining gaps are diffuse per-visit cost rather than surplus work, with one
architectural exception: during Sweep, Tactix maintains both the sparse watch
lists and the dense occurrence index, where Kissat's sweep has only the latter.
Removing that duplication needs a dense propagation path Tactix does not have.

\section{Limitations}

Rank~31 remains the worst instance at 671~s against 262~s, and its gap is search
trajectory rather than implementation: it takes 2.7$\times$ the conflicts. That
is a separate problem and it is not closed.

Two documentation budgets are over: \code{PROGRESS.md} at 3{,}773 lines against
350 and \code{docs/implementation-notes.md} at 591 against 560. Compaction was
not requested.

Evidence is in
\code{benchmarks/reports/2026-09-02-unit-cost-and-fidelity-stack.md},
sections~3.12 to~3.21, with campaigns \code{5010} through \code{5054}.
