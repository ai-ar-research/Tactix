\section{Objective and outcome}

The objective is to close every implementation gap against Kissat: every case
where Tactix does the same work and takes longer. Being within 10\,\% on
Tier~D Ranks~1--40 is the floor, not the finish. B28+C5 is the regression gate
and N1--N4 are preserved.

\textbf{Outcome.} Ranks~1--40 stand at \textbf{8{,}535~s}, or
\textbf{1.112$\times$} Kissat's 7{,}675.1~s, from 8{,}781.1~s at the start of
this work; B28+C5 improved from 0.495 to \textbf{0.463}. Ten repairs landed,
each gated 40/40 valid with 10{,}000 fuzz cases and zero failures on both
legs, and every one behaviour-preserving with identical solver counters. The
net per-rule gap fell from roughly 500~s to \textbf{428~s}.

The goal is \emph{not} met: 428~s of inprocessing gap remains. What this
session can now say precisely, which it could not before, is what that 428~s
is made of.

\section{The result that matters}

Every remaining rule gap is unit cost. Tactix performs \emph{less} work than
Kissat on every paired counter but one, while being slower on every rule but
two.

Read the last two columns together. No rule can be closed by dosing it down,
because none is doing surplus work; and no rule is buying its time with extra
simplification. Strengthen has no honest phase counterpart, since Kissat
subsumes inside \code{eliminate}: paired properly, BVE+Strengthen is 372.3~s
against 230.2~s, a ratio of 1.62 and the largest single pairing left.

This is now reproducible in one command. \code{tools/bench/rule\_gap\_table.py}
prints the table from a gate campaign plus a Kissat statistics baseline, and
campaign \code{5064} supplies the latter --- the pinned Kissat run under
\code{-v} on all forty ranks, which is the only way it prints its full
statistics. Phase times and counters come from different campaigns on purpose,
since \code{-v} perturbs timing.

\section{What was repaired}

\textbf{A diagnostic that shipped} (170.6~s, the largest single repair). BVE
ran an occurrence audit on every candidate elimination whose only consumers
are trace calls that are empty inline functions in a production build.

\textbf{Factor} (281.8~s $\to$ 238~s). Per scanned entry Tactix took two random
cache misses where Kissat takes one, because Kissat's skip mark is a clause
header bit read from a line it already loads. Moving both marks there made the
occurrence identifier dead, which let the watch record halve. Its remainder
pool also moved from a map of bucket vectors to an open-addressed table.

\textbf{Congruence's doubled ITE gates.} Kissat canonicalises the triple so one
gate covers a relation and its output-negated twin. Rank~22's gate count went
from 10.48\,M to 7.10\,M against Kissat's 6.91\,M with merges unchanged.

\textbf{Five quadratic removals} across substitution, the root-satisfied binary
scans, the binary duplicate test and the dense occurrence lookup.

\section{What was refuted}

Six attempts measured neutral, slower, or unsound and were reverted rather than
banked: Factor's local tick accumulation; guarding fourteen trace-only
computations the compiler already eliminates; BVE's per-clause vector hoist;
capping the binary copy count; bulk-stripping an eliminated variable's binaries
(slower, and a first version was unsound); and deriving the large occurrence
index from the ordered one.

Three structural hypotheses also died to measurement. Propagation is 42\,\% of
runtime and looked 1.5$\times$ slower on Rank~31, but that instance runs
2.7$\times$ Kissat's conflicts; at equal counts the rates are within 9\,\%.
Sweep, which an earlier report named the largest gap on one rank's evidence, is
124~s \emph{faster} than Kissat tier-wide. And the claim that dense-mode rules
pay to maintain sparse watches too is wrong: on Rank~21 that machinery is
0.05\,\% against Kissat's roughly 7\,\%.

\section{Three measurement corrections}

Each of these changed a conclusion, and they are the most transferable output
of the session.

\textbf{Allocation shape.} Early A/B campaigns ran at four CPUs and 16~GiB,
which the benchmark runner documents as the \emph{debug} contract; the gates use
twelve and 48~GiB. The two-word Factor watch record was refuted under those
conditions and, retried correctly, proved worth 2.8~s.

\textbf{The tier total cannot arbitrate.} One repair measured a consistent
$-1.4$~s in paired repeats and returned a gate \textbf{72.8~s slower} with all
forty conflict counts identical. Its noise band is far wider than any single
repair; paired same-node repeats must decide.

\textbf{A patched reference that compiles can still be wrong.} Instrumenting
Kissat to expose its vivification propagations, I declared the propagate
candidate \code{unsigned} where the real signature takes \code{struct clause *}.
It compiled, ran, reported plausible counters --- and answered SATISFIABLE on an
UNSAT instance. Any patched reference must now match the pristine build on the
verdict and one pre-existing counter before a new number is read from it.

\section{Correctness}

The proof and model checks of all ten gate pairs run in this session have
finished: 500 proof checks and 560 model checks, every one exit code~0, with no
instance whose verdict disagreed with its expected result. N2 holds for every
commit landed, independently of the counter identity used to judge the repairs.

\section{Where this leaves the work}

The 428~s is diffuse. Within each rule the rule's own code is a minority of its
time --- on Rank~21 \code{bve.cpp} is 9.3~s of BVE's 35.2~s --- and the shared
machinery it charges into is structurally faithful to Kissat. Two shared paths
carry measured gaps and neither is a local fix: dense index construction is
4.14~s against roughly 1.4~s and is call-count driven, since Tactix rebuilds
once per rule application where Kissat connects once per dense phase and lets
the rules share it; and collection is about 5.2~s against 1.95~s.

The sharpest single number remains BVE's: on Rank~21 it performs 97.8\,M
resolutions where Kissat performs 180.8\,M, eliminates 3.5\,\% more variables
from a third of the attempts, and is 3.2$\times$ slower per resolution.

Two documentation budgets are over: \code{PROGRESS.md} at 3{,}800 lines against
350 and \code{docs/implementation-notes.md} at 591 against 560. Compaction was
not requested.

Evidence is in
\code{benchmarks/reports/2026-09-02-unit-cost-and-fidelity-stack.md},
sections~3.12 to~3.25, with campaigns \code{5010} through \code{5064}.
