\section*{Executive summary}
The revisit preserved the shipped solver trajectory while making the strategy
boundary substantially clearer.  DefaultPolicy now contains the CDCL/search
strategy and composes a dedicated DefaultInprocessScheduler for macro phase
order, clocks, budgets, watermarks, delays, cursors, telemetry, and Factor/BVE
coupling.  Factor owns a neutral selection interface, and Factor and Substitute
now report facts through kernel events instead of importing or invoking policy
code.  The high-volume source commentary was removed: comment-only lines in the
three original hotspots plus the extracted scheduler fell from 1,542 to zero.

The refactor is trajectory-identical on 399 independently checked comparisons,
passes the complete Debug and Release suites, and has no detected structural
performance regression.  The recommended next step is to resume the Kissat
default-parity campaign one N4 row at a time through the new scheduler boundary,
starting with SP-COMPACT-1 unless another row is explicitly selected.

\section{Implementation and design decisions}
\subsection{Composed inprocessing scheduler}
The new \texttt{src/policies/default\_inprocess.hpp} owns the complete mutable
macro-scheduling state and exposes a single test/preset surface through
\texttt{DefaultPolicy::inprocess()}.  DefaultPolicy retains branching,
restart/reduce, mode switching, rephase/reorder, glue/decision-rate observations,
and the search loop.  The default-policy implementation shrank from 2,640 lines
to 1,870 lines including the extracted header; its public header shrank from
586 to 148 lines.

\subsection{Proof-rule versus strategy boundary}
\texttt{FactorSelection} is defined by the Factor rule module.
\texttt{FactorMatchPolicy} adapts that rule-owned interface into the policy
system.  The kernel holds only a scoped non-owning pointer to the neutral
interface.  Factor emits fresh-variable events and Substitute emits substituted
and retirement events.  Policies consume those events and apply heuristic
reactions; the C API deliberately discards them as neutral advice.  A new CTest
source guard rejects policy imports and heuristic calls from every macro formula
rule while retaining the four search-micro exceptions.

\subsection{Comment cleanup}
The cleanup deleted comment-only prose from
\texttt{default\_policy.cpp}, \texttt{default\_policy.hpp}, and
\texttt{kernel.hpp}; the extracted scheduler also contains no comment-only
blocks.  Executable tokens in the mechanical pass were unchanged.  Concise
inline annotations and the calculus, implementation notes, design documents,
tests, and parity ledger remain the owners of invariants and provenance.

\section{Limitations and incidents}
Three early identity submissions (1592221, 1592222, 1592224) were intentionally
cancelled because the newly extended validator mishandled empty SAT models and
drat-trim's intentional nonzero exit for trivial UNSAT.  Diagnostic job 1592225
retained one case, showed \texttt{s VERIFIED}, and proved the two proof files
byte-identical.  The corrected gate then passed.  These were harness defects,
not solver failures.

The Tier B array was submitted with an unnecessary throttle of eight.  An
attempt to remove it raced with completion; all tasks had already been admitted,
with only two running and none pending.  Future arrays should be unthrottled
unless the runner or experimental design specifically requires a cap.

The implementation creates a semantic scheduler component, but its method
definitions remain in \texttt{default\_policy.cpp}.  A future purely mechanical
translation-unit split is possible; it should be undertaken only if navigation
remains a problem, under the same identity gate.  No scheduling default, rule
identifier, C API, proof format, or output schema changed.
