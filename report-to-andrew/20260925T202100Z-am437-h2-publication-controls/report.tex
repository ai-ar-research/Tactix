\section*{Outcome}
The new 16-clause, one-retained-reason H2 reproducer rules out another
simple no-option DSR repair. Nine pinned checker-only variants tested
publishing implied unit 19, publishing the local 18/19 closure, omitting the
reason-clause deletion, and repeating that deletion. None verified under
the frozen canonical no-option \texttt{dsr-trim}; all outcomes and raw logs
are preserved in \href{../../benchmarks/reports/2026-09-25-am437-h2-unit-publication-controls.md}{AM437}.
No solver, proof emitter, checker binary or acceptance route changed.

\section*{What the result means}
The exact ASCII re-encoding reproduces the binary baseline's backward
watch-pointer failure and forward SR-obligation failure. Publishing unit 19
before deleting input clause 15 can make that deletion effective in the
checker: a second identical deletion then fails because there is no matching
clause. But the explicit unit remains in the checked formula, and a later SR
witness still fails against a different clause obligation. Omitting the
deletion also leaves the baseline failure. Thus neither clause-population
alignment by one publication nor this local closure is a complete proof
derivation. These controls corroborate the earlier original-H2 results; they
do not establish that the canonical contract is impossible in general.

The diagnostic \texttt{--delete-implied-units} contract verifies this H2
proof through Trestle, yet archived real-solver controls show it can also
reject proofs accepted by the no-option contract. It is not a monotone
relaxation. A production switch would require an explicit contract decision
and broad positive/negative, original-CNF and cumulative gate revalidation.
That choice has been put to the user; the no-option route remains canonical
until decided.
