\section{Representation and boundary preservation}
The prior implementation stored every materialized antecedent and every
generated resolvent in its own \code{std::vector}.  Rank 27 performs 1.699
million BVE applications and 64.243 million resolution attempts on the
accepted trajectory, making allocator ownership an avoidable part of the hot
path.  Kissat instead uses raw reusable stacks for the same lifetime.

Tactix now keeps four reusable payload pools in the existing per-rule
\code{BveDefScratch}: antecedent literals, absorbed root-unit proof IDs,
resolvent literals, and resolvent LRAT chains.  Antecedent and resolvent records
carry a pool pointer plus 32-bit offset and length.  Offsets remain valid across
vector relocation.  Pools are cleared only when the next candidate begins,
after the prior candidate has committed or declined.  A satisfied large
antecedent rewinds both captured pool starts; tautological and oversized
resolvents rewind their literal start before the old control flow continues.

This is an allocation representation change, not a new rule.  The existing
antecedent admission, gate classification, pair traversal, resolvent-size and
growth bounds, proof transaction, elimination reconstruction, and policy
schedule are untouched.  Every registered BVE entry already owns the persistent
scratch; a debug assertion makes that lifetime requirement explicit.  The
public N1 operation remains complete, N2 retains independently checked models
and proofs, and neither N3 nor N4 is weakened.
