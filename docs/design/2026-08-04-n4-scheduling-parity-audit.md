# N4 scheduling-parity audit — the full ledger

**Status:** audit artifact, 2026-08-04. **FROZEN on landing — never edited.**
**Why it exists:** plan rev 80 made N4 (the strategy floor) a non-negotiable and named the
**scheduling-parity ledger** as its operational test. This is the first pass of that ledger, and the
provenance for the LIVING register in `docs/implementation-notes.md` §13.
**Companion:** the *why* is diagnosed in `docs/design/2026-08-04-scheduling-sophistication-diagnosis.md`;
the staged remediation is **Track N4** in `PROGRESS.md`.

**Method.** Eight parallel auditors, one per rule family plus one for the global slot, each comparing our
scheduling against **kissat 4.0.4 AND cadical 3.0** mechanism by mechanism, with file:line on all three
sides. **216 mechanisms audited, 132 gaps.** Severity: 27 high, 53 medium, 15 low (the remainder are
WE_MATCH rows kept only where the parity is load-bearing or corrects a recorded belief).

**Known limitation, stated by the audit itself (theme T7):** this pass was organised by OUR rule
families, so it is structurally blind to mechanisms we never knew to look for. The diagnosis names the
fix — regenerate the row list from the REFERENCE's option set (`kissat/src/options.h`,
`cadical/src/options.hpp`) rather than from our own recall — and that is Track N4 item **N4-A3**. Read
this ledger as a lower bound on the gap, not a complete inventory.

**Reading the verdicts.** `WE_MATCH` parity · `WE_EXCEED` finer than both · `WE_ARE_CRUDER` mechanism
present but cruder · `WE_LACK_IT` reference has it, we do not · `REFERENCE_LACKS_IT` we invented
something neither reference has (a liability unless measured — see theme T4 and the Factor density
gate, which measured worse than having no gate at all).

« WHAT THE GAPS HAVE IN COMMON »

Eight auditors, ~150 mechanisms, and the failures are not independent. Five patterns carry almost all of them, and two of the four candidate patterns need restating before they are true.

---

### T1. We port mechanisms; we drop the WIRES between them. (candidate (i) — STRONGLY SUPPORTED, and it is the master pattern)

Almost every *intra-rule* mechanism is faithful: SET_EFFORT_LIMIT's shape and floor, the BVE bound predicate and ramp, factordelay's truncation, the vivify 7:3 split and delay ladder, the restart EMA warm-up and reluctant sequence, the mode ticks/conflicts alternation, the reduce keep test, the dynamic tier computation, Factor's heap and re-scoring, the search-loop check order. Where we exceed, it is also intra-rule: `bve_min_bound`, Factor's triple watermark, BIG-head candidate filters, within-pass composition, the walk unit correction, the measured bump-reasons rate.

Every high-severity gap is a *cross-mechanism* wire:

- `bve_level_` → `factor_bound` — the one coupling BOTH references implement identically; fully plumbed on our side and fed a static 0.
- `k.tier2` → Vivify — computed, correct, shipped ON, consumed by Reduce, and Vivify reads a hardcoded 6.
- C1/C2 completeness → BVE's bound advance — kissat will not relax elimination while subsumption has unfinished business; we do.
- Congruence → Substitute — adjacent and conditional in both references, four rules apart and unconditional here.
- TransitiveReduce's failed literal → Probe — sound to defer, but Probe is at slot position 6 and V4 at 10, so the deferral cannot be honoured within the phase.
- X4 → `bve_retry` — cadical refuses to probe what BVE is about to eliminate; we spend probes there.
- Factor → the branching heuristic — both references demote fresh extension variables unconditionally; ours defaults OFF, so every variable Factor adds becomes a MOST-preferred VMTF decision.
- Reduce cadence ↔ deletion fraction — one ramp is a function of the other's counter; we shipped two independently-chosen stand-ins and wired both to one env var so they cannot even be measured apart.
- BVE ↔ probing cadence — one interval where the references keep two coupled clocks.

The recognisable sub-shape, named in six separate rows: **we own the mechanism, the field is named after the reference mechanism, and nothing writes it.** `Kernel::factor_bound`, `VIVIFY_TIER2` vs `k.tier2`, `Kernel::factor_cand_rounds`=0, `Kernel::factor_demote_fresh`=false, `Kernel::bve_cost_limit`=0, `TACTIX_REORDER`=0, `Kernel::bce_occ_limit`=0, `stats.transitive_ticks` (maintained, not metered), `used_glue[1]` (written, never read), `arena_mark` (written for closures, never read). This is worse than a missing mechanism because grep finds the mechanism and reports parity.

Why the asymmetry exists is architecturally legible: a rule is one directory with one contract, so intra-rule fidelity has an owner; a coupling lives between two rules and belongs to the policy, and the policy grew as a list of independently-ported entries.

---

### T2. Repetition, not dose, is the damage axis — and we govern dose. (NOT in the candidate list; the single largest cluster)

Twelve-plus rows are the same defect in different rules: **both references make round k+1 cost O(new material); ours costs O(whole formula).** The mechanism is always one of three: a per-item new-material mark, a resumption cursor, or a leftover/attempt bit.

Missing marks: `flags->subsume` (C1+C2), per-literal `factor`, per-literal `block` (BCE), per-variable `ternary` (X2), per-clause `instantiated` (X4), cadical's `propfixed` (Probe), the slot-level `variables_{eliminate,subsume}` / `last.elim.marked` / `last.inprobe.reductions`, BVE's round-level watermark and its endgame stop.
Missing resumption: Vivify's `Clause::vivify` bit, cadical's `c->transred`, cadical's persistent `probes` stack, C1/C2's completeness-driven flag reactivation, Factor's cross-call attempt bit.

Two consequences compound, and this is the part that makes the cluster dangerous rather than merely wasteful:

1. **A binding budget plus a fixed order plus no resumption makes a whole candidate class dead code.** Vivify re-tries the same head of the size-sorted list every round forever; Probe/Backbone truncate the same high-index tail of the variable range on every instance where the dose runs out; BCE spends its ledger on literals 0,1,2,… so what it does is a function of the DIMACS variable numbering; V4 (once its meter binds) will never reach the high half of the BIG. Ordering is not a micro-optimisation here — under a cutting budget, **ordering IS the scheduler**, because it selects which candidates exist.
2. **The measured damage tracks ROUNDS, not work.** j3037's conflicts track BCE round count (48/37/27/0 → 7.60M/4.47M/1.63M/1.28M) while BCE is 0.1% of the solve. We diagnosed this correctly, wrote the right control (`TACTIX_SLOT_MAX_APPLIES`), labelled it diagnostic, and shipped the effort ledger — the control we had already measured to be the wrong one.

The delay ladder is our one repetition suppressor and it is gated on `!e.meter`, so precisely the metered batch rules that repeat (BCE, X2, C1, C2, X4) can never back off.

### T3. Our budgets have the right FORMULA and the wrong METER. (candidate (iv) — REFUTED as stated; replace it)

The claim "our budgets are not scaled off search progress the way per-mille-of-ticks schemes are" is false and should be struck. `permille × (search_ticks − anchor)`, the `mineffort` 10M floor, the spend+delta form and the once-per-call placement are ported faithfully for every metered entry, and where the references disagree (kissat's family-shared anchor vs cadical's per-technique anchor) we took cadical's, the more conservative. Two rates were corrected by measurement against reference constants (X2 20→8‰, +1 solve on Tier C) and one against a UNIT audit (walk 50→560‰, because kissat scales from cache lines and spends in per-entry steps).

The real defect is three distinct properties of the METER, and conflating them is why several dose experiments came back inert or negative:

- **Wrong currency.** V4 meters CANDIDATES, so a twentyfold rate cut is bit-identical — the ledger cannot bind while reading as satisfied. C1/C2 meter candidates where the references meter partner-clause visits — up to 1000× coarser, so "100‰" buys wildly more work and the limit binds about once per solve. C2's root-flush pre-pass and BCE/Vivify/X2's O(arena) setups are charged to nothing at all.
- **No privacy.** `meter_ticks = k.stats.ticks` is shared by Probe, Backbone, Vivify and Instantiate plus every `propagate_apply`. Both references keep private per-technique counters, and that privacy is *why* their per-mille numbers mean anything. Ours produces order-dependent silent starvation with no counter that distinguishes "starved" from "nothing to do" — an arm that looks scheduled and does nothing, i.e. the byte-identical-artefacts failure mode at the scheduling layer.
- **Wrong placement.** The ceiling is frozen for all entries at phase entry, before any of them runs, and the anchor is advanced even for entries that are then declined — so accrued entitlement is destroyed, which is the exact inverse of cadical's `NAMEthresh`, whose whole point is to let entitlement accumulate until the dose covers the setup cost.

Corollary for the evidence base: **every prior negative measured on a rule whose meter has one of these three defects must be re-derived.** That specifically covers the V4 ticks-meter arm, the C1/C2 mid-round-budget arm, the V4 early-exit arm, and any per-entry permille sweep on a ticks-metered rule.

### T4. Static instance gates substituting for per-application quality bounds. (candidate (iii) — SUPPORTED, with a causal mechanism)

Confirmed and sharper than stated. The references' governors are uniformly per-application: a growth bound, an occurrence limit per pivot, a clause-size limit per resolvent, an effort budget, a best-first heap. **Neither reference contains a single formula-SHAPE admission test** for BVE, Factor, blocking or vivification.

Ours: the binary-density gate (Factor's short-circuit AND BVE's dose gate), `factor_scope_percent` (dormant), `VIVIFY_CLSLIM=100`, `CONG_ARITY_LIMIT=64`, X4's compile-time occlim/clslim. Four of these are the hand-designed separator family the project has already measured dead four times (L5, L15, S4 §2, the 2026-07-30 reference study), and the density gate is measured **worse than no gate at all** (86.42 vs 34.59 against a 32.25 oracle, costing three solves). For BVE it is not tuning at all: below the ratio, BVE never runs on that instance — a permanent rule-off shipped as a default, which the standing baseline forbids.

The causal chain is the part worth recording: **the invented instance gate exists BECAUSE the per-application bound was never wired.** Factor's only reference-sanctioned acceptance governor is `reduction > bound`; ours is pinned at 0 and therefore accepts any gain ≥ 1; with no working brake per application, someone reached for a brake per instance. Same shape for `CONG_ARITY_LIMIT=64`, which stands in for the effort budget V6 does not have — and a cap loses gates silently where a budget spends best-first. So T4 is a downstream consequence of T1, not an independent taste for gates.

### T5. Ramps we lack are exactly the ramps keyed on ANOTHER mechanism's counter. (candidate (ii) — SUPPORTED ONLY IN A REFINED FORM)

"We use static constants where references ramp" is too coarse — it is refuted by the ramps we do have: BVE's bound doubling (correctly completion-gated), factordelay's decade ladder, the vivify irredundant delay ladder, the closure delay ladder, the superlinear mode/rephase/inprocess intervals, the doubling tier-recompute interval, the growing focused restart limit.

Partition by the ramp's KEY and the pattern is exact:

- Keyed on the rule's OWN counter → we have it (bve_level_ on BVE completions, delay ladders on the rule's own yield, intervals on the rule's own round count).
- Keyed on ANOTHER mechanism's counter → we lack it, every time: Factor's bound ← the elimination bound; reduce's deletion fraction ← the reduction count that also sets the interval; backbone's round cap ← the backbone computation count; C1/C2's redundant-candidate eligibility ← reduce's dynamic keep limits (cadical); vivify's candidate bands ← the glue quantiles.

So T5 is T1 again, seen from the time axis: **a ramp whose key belongs to another mechanism IS a coupling.** That also explains the one place we ramp less than the reference on our own key (BVE ceiling 8 vs 16): that was a measured trade taken under a ladder that advances up to 3× too fast per phase, i.e. the ceiling is an artefact of a coupling defect one statement away.

### T6. The schedule is emergent, not authored. (not in the candidate list; cheapest to fix, four defects deep)

Both references' phase orders are argued in their source: kissat fixes probe.c's sequence by argument; cadical states that its BIG-root probe selection *requires* decompose to have run. Ours is `strcmp` over rule names plus two `move_to_back` calls. The consequences are not stylistic:

ELS (Substitute, 'S') runs after Backbone ('B') and Probe ('P'), so the BIG they walk is un-decomposed and whole SCCs have no root. Vivify ('V') runs after TransitiveReduce ('T'), opposite to both references, *and* lands last in the shared-meter queue, so the ordering accident and the starvation channel compound. BCE ('B') runs first, inverting both of cadical's stated orderings (subsume-before-block, block-immediately-before-BVE). Congruence and its consumer Substitute are four rules apart, so HTR, Instantiate, Probe and Strengthen spend their budgets on a formula carrying live a↔b duplication. TransitiveReduce's deferred failed literal cannot be picked up by Probe until the next phase. The two orderings we got right are the two we explicitly authored (`move_to_back("BVE")`, `move_to_back("Factor")`) — and one of them is worth 222 s → 4.2 s on clique.

X4-before-BVE is currently correct **by alphabetical luck**; rename a rule and it moves.

### T7. Our reference-reading is the load-bearing risk in this audit. (meta-theme, both directions)

The audit found reference mechanisms that are dead, inert, off-by-default, or invisible to grep — and we have been burned in both directions:

- Constants adopted from code that never executes: cadical 3.0.0's `ternary()` computes `steps_limit = stats.ticks.ternary − limit` = −DELTA and breaks out of the round loop immediately, so **`ternaryeffort=8‰` is not evidence about anything**. Our 8‰ measured well and is an OWN constant.
- Deviations recorded against options that are off: vivify.hpp deviation (2) and the D-VIVIFY-SCHED row claim both references retry within a candidate — kissat has no retry at all and cadical's `vivifyretry` defaults 0. A recorded deficit that does not exist.
- Justifications citing uncompiled code: reduce.cpp cites kissat's `TIEDX`/`focusedtiers` for our tier source; that form is inside `#if 0`, and kissat's live macros read tier1 from the FOCUSED and tier2 from the STABLE histogram. We actually follow cadical.
- Mechanisms missed because the option name is token-pasted: cadical's `factorthresh`/`vivifythresh`/`ternarythresh` are live and a prior study concluded they were dead. Any N4 row citing that study must be re-derived.
- Dead mechanisms we correctly did NOT port: kissat's `flags.transitive`/`transitivekeep` (never set true) and its `complete` in eliminate (shadowed at :466, so the bound doubles on every call and the drop branch is dead).

Discipline that follows: an N4 row may cite a reference option only with its DEFAULT and the EXECUTION PATH that reaches it. And several ledger rows are currently doc/code drift (a deleted `TACTIX_FACTOR_BOUND_COUPLE` arm, a BCE occ-limit comment asserting 100 when it is 0, a claim that kissat's probe phase contains BVE, a claim that composition matches kissat's `resize_factoring`) — each of which would turn a future A/B into an identical-artefacts result.

### Net reading for N4

N4 is **not** met today, and the reason is structural rather than a list of missing features. Rule-locally we are at or above both references on most mechanisms and genuinely exceed them on several (per-variable min-bound caching, BIG-head candidate filters, within-pass composition, a real BCE effort budget where cadical has none, an X2 output governor implementing the intent cadical's option only names, a corrected walk currency, a measured bump-reasons rate). What is missing is the layer above the rules: **the couplings (T1/T5), the repetition suppressors (T2), an honest meter (T3), and an authored order (T6)** — plus, in the places where those four are absent, invented instance gates standing in for them (T4).

That is good news for cost: T1, T5 and T6 are mostly one-line or data-only changes, T3's worst instance is one statement moved, and T2 is a small number of bitsets and cursors. It is bad news for evidence: a large fraction of the project's banked negatives were measured through a broken meter, a frozen ceiling, a burnt anchor, or an emergent order — so the honest closing statement of this ledger is that **several existing "measured dead" verdicts are not yet safe to rely on**, and the first order of business is the P0 block, which makes the subsequent measurements mean what they say.
