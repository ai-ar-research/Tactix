# SP-VIVIFY-3 closure

**Outcome:** implemented and retained, but **ships off**. The mechanism is
correctness-clean and live; the pre-registered Tier C gate rejects it as a
default.

## Mechanism and boundary

Commit `a7d7a4b` ports CaDiCaL's `vivifyflush`: before a canonical Vivify round
spends propagations, candidates are ordered by their occurrence-ranked literal
sequence within four separate bands (irredundant and redundant tiers 1--3).
A clause is deleted when the preceding retained sequence is its prefix. This is
C1 subsumption embedded in the canonical C3 round, so the proof delta is only
the ordinary clause deletion.

The N1 surface is unchanged. `vivify_candidates()` and per-clause instances
remain complete, pure, and independent of the schedule optimization. The final
shipped default is `vivify_flush=false`; `TACTIX_VIVIFY_FLUSH=1` enables the
retained experimental arm.

## Correctness and reach

- Release suite: 60/60 after the default was switched off. The pre-benchmark
  assertion/address/undefined-sanitizer focus passed 6/6.
- Final enabled-pin LRAT fuzz job `1594317`: 10,000 cases, zero failures, 9,827
  checked verdicts; `VivifyFlush` fired in 1,416 cases.
- Targeted LRAT stress job `1594318`: 10,000 cases, zero failures, 8,443 checked
  verdicts, 2,784,200 forced Vivify applications; `VivifyFlush` fired in 1,942
  cases. Harness self-test `1594319` passed.
- Focused tests prove that the instance surface still exposes both clauses,
  enabled flush deletes before propagation and emits the proof deletion, the
  off arm retains both, and no deletion crosses irredundancy or tier bands.

All three benchmark result files report `correctness.clean=true`: no verdict
mismatch, invalid model, invalid certificate, checker give-up, or unverified
scored answer.

## Same-window benchmark

All runs used commit `a7d7a4b`, binary SHA-256
`01647eba79bbb9cb75868c281ce54ef3150cf8b420ca08753e3de4e6ec774998`,
partition `cpu-dense-hwulab-q`, and **no array throttle**. Enabled and disabled
arms used the same binary; the disabled command set `TACTIX_VIVIFY_FLUSH=0`.
Kissat and CaDiCaL ran in the same arrays. Tier A/B used one core and 8 GB per
task; Tier C used two cores and 16 GB. Tier-list hashes were `7c0e15d5...e9b7a5f`
(A), `51308660...c6bcef` (B), and `7c5548b9...4786b71` (C).

| Tier / run | flush enabled | flush disabled | Kissat | CaDiCaL | paired reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A `20260808-234549-A-vivflush` | **200/200, 0.52** | 199/200, 0.67 | 200/200, 0.45 | 199/200, 0.75 | 199 common solves: 0.839 geometric-mean ratio; one instance flips the aggregate sign |
| B `20260808-234549-B-vivflush` | **28/28, 52.48** | 26/28, 69.52 | 28/28, 10.06 | 27/28, 40.54 | 26 common solves: 1.214 ratio; removing two gained solves flips the sign |
| C `20260808-234836-C-vivflush` | 19/30, 1047.71 | **21/30, 883.25** | 29/30, 249.42 | 28/30, 379.15 | 19 common solves: 1.186 ratio; the loss survives removing any four instances |

Ratios are enabled/disabled geometric means over commonly solved instances.
On Tier C the disabled arm additionally solves `StConn_8_32` (UNSAT, 373.363 s)
and `j3037_9_rggt_b` (SAT, 361.324 s), while enabled times out on both. The
largest mover is `j3037_9_rggt_b`, but five instances must be removed to reverse
the aggregate sign. This is a broad loss, not a one-instance trajectory cliff.

The mechanism is substantial: enabled flushing removed 107,779 candidates on
72 Tier A cases, 1,115,949 on 27 Tier B cases, and 2,061,500 on 19 Tier C cases.
It is therefore neither inert nor under-exercised.

## Decision

Do not adopt CaDiCaL's flush as Tactix's default. Tier A and B benefits do not
override the larger, robust Tier C regression. Keeping the implementation behind
an explicit on arm closes the engineering gap without weakening the current
Kissat-default campaign or mixing the scheduling decision into the per-clause
proof-rule interface. The next ledger item is `SP-SWEEP-3`.
