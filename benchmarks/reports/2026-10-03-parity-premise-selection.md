# Certified XOR premise recognition and bounded selection

## Implementation and soundness

`parity_premises.hpp` consumes an explicitly identified clause snapshot. It groups exact two- through four-literal clauses by distinct positive variable scope and records the first occurrence of each sign mask. An equation is exposed only if every forbidden-assignment mask of the required parity is present. Missing clauses produce no equation. Both complete parities are retained when present; together they certify contradiction. Duplicate clause contents do not inflate coverage; duplicate or zero occurrence IDs invalidate the snapshot. Repeated-variable clauses are ignored rather than normalized into guessed premises. This is separate from SAT-only heuristic recognition.

`parity_selection.hpp` performs bounded sparse GF(2) elimination in a caller-selected ascending or descending pivot order. Every intermediate row records the symmetric difference of its source-equation indices. XORing exactly those original equations recovers its variables and right-hand side. Width and provenance-size allowances suppress oversized combinations, and the action-local work allowance stops discovery with only valid prefix consequences. No global budget is introduced. Work counts explicit row/provenance visits, not elapsed time or a complete instruction count. These are internal helpers with explicit limits, not DefaultPolicy parameter choices or a new legal action surface.

The collector and selector mutate no kernel, proof or model. Callers must supply real live clauses and a valid external-variable mapping under an identified snapshot epoch. The supplied occurrence ID is not blindly taken from `kernel_binary_id`: `BinaryIdMap::get` can return sentinel 1 when exact values/keys are not retained. A live adapter must assign and stream its own stable snapshot occurrence identities or use an actually retained identity service. It must not label this sentinel as a source proof ID.

## Fixed validation

The [retained run](../runs/20261003-parity-premise-selection/) binds source/checker hashes and exact build/test/check commands. Fixed tests cover both parities at every supported arity, literal permutation, every one-clause omission, duplicate content and identities, invalid literal/identity inputs, conflicting complete encodings, clause/group limits, width/provenance limits and all effort cut points for the cancellation fixture. Each returned selection is independently recomputed from its listed source rows in the test.

The end-to-end fixture begins with the actual 16 CNF clauses, assigns and records occurrence IDs, recognizes four complete equations, selects their cancellation, and invokes the previously extracted proof engine. Descending elimination finds x iff y in 44 charged work units; ascending elimination does not find a short consequence on this ordering. That is expected heuristic incompleteness, not unsoundness. The proof emits 274 prefix additions and 16 fresh proof helpers. The collector's deterministic ordering differs from the previous manually supplied equation ordering, so the proof counts differ; no performance advantage is claimed.

Canonical witness-free DSR conversion plus DRAT/CakeLPR checking passes. Forward every-addition DRAT and independently checked all-addition LRAT/CakeLPR pass, including exact deletions. The retained elaborator checks that no prefix hint uses the disjoint closing gadget's original premise IDs. An unsupported unit is independently rejected. Clause bodies, selected equations and exact source occurrence lists are retained in `premises.jsonl`; the full certificate supplies the proof-event trace. This is fixed helper evidence, not a public action's completed semantic provenance implementation.
